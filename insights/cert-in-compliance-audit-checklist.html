What a CERT-In compliance audit checks, step by step

5 min read · Compliance guides

CERT-In, India's Computer Emergency Response Team, empanels auditors to assess organizations' cybersecurity posture — often required for regulated sectors, government empanelment, or specific compliance directions. Here's what that process generally looks at, and how it relates to ISO 27001.

Step 1: Scoping and documentation review

The audit starts by defining which systems, networks, and data are in scope, followed by a review of your existing security policies — access control, data handling, incident response, and backup procedures. If these don't exist yet, this is usually the first gap identified.

Step 2: Network and infrastructure assessment

Auditors typically review network architecture, firewall configurations, and exposure of internet-facing systems. This often includes vulnerability scanning and, depending on scope, penetration testing to identify exploitable weaknesses before they're found some other way.

Step 3: Access control and identity management

Who has access to what, and why, is a recurring focus. Auditors look for role-based access controls, whether former employees' access is actually revoked promptly, and whether privileged accounts (like system administrators) have additional oversight.

Step 4: Data protection and backup verification

This covers how sensitive data is stored, encrypted, and backed up — and critically, whether backups are actually tested for successful restoration, not just scheduled.

Step 5: Incident response readiness

Auditors typically check whether a documented incident response plan exists, whether staff know their role in it, and whether past incidents (if any) were logged and reported appropriately — including CERT-In's own incident reporting requirements where applicable.

How this overlaps with ISO 27001

If your organization already holds ISO 27001 certification, a meaningful portion of this ground is already covered — access control, risk assessment, and incident response are core requirements of both frameworks. ISO 27001 doesn't automatically satisfy a CERT-In audit's specific technical checks, like vulnerability scanning, but it puts you in a considerably stronger starting position than an organization with no formal security management system at all.

Practical takeaway

Treat a CERT-In audit and ISO 27001 as complementary rather than interchangeable: one is a compliance audit tied to specific technical checks, the other is an ongoing management system. Businesses that build ISO 27001 first tend to find CERT-In audits considerably less disruptive, since most of the underlying documentation and controls already exist.
